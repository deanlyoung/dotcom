Building Trust for an Agentic Lifestyle

During Halloween week, they handled 320 million trips and orders across Uber and Uber Eats. Out of 320 million transactions, only 4 rides and 500 Uber Eats orders were placed through Al agents.

DoorDash reported almost the exact same thing the week before. Honestly, I'm not surprised at all. Companies like Uber and DoorDash have spent the last decade and massive amounts of product R&D obsessing over every tap of their visual Ul and UX, and the result is a fast, frictionless experience that gives users direct visual control over their orders.

https://lnkd.in/p/gQgk4Gj8

Interesting hypothesis built on arguably incorrect assumptions. I wouldn't say their product teams obsess over making the process seamless. Their UIs bombard you with noise and dark patterns from obsessive A:B testing to drive very specific and incrementally better metrics. Using these apps is still less friction than driving somewhere or calling a cab, but they are by no means as simple or seamless as they could be if that were the main metric/goal.

There could be (and likely are) other reasons agentic ordering has not taken over. I agree with the point that a chat app is not the ideal UI/UX for these things and maybe not until they establish more trust that they can execute properly without needing a UI to get what you expect. The equivalent of WYSIWYG but for a black box. However, the difference is that web development took a specific skill set, but "seeing" what is happening inside an AI agent to give you confidence you don't need to handhold it through your request is a leap of faith that needs to be overcome.
